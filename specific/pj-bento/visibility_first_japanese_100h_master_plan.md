- Visible workplace Japanese progress comes first.
- Future JLPT N2 usefulness matters, but dedicated N2 prep does not.
- Speaking and listening are the primary modes.
- Reading is support-only: transcripts, phrase capture, and light checkpointing.
- English is accepted at work, so Japanese is being used to build trust and visible progress.
- The learner is a Chinese L1 speaker, so kanji recognition is easier than average.
- Dedicated katakana pronunciation drilling is low value.
- The target register is staff-level, direct, and accountable rather than soft or ceremonial.

### High-priority workplace situations

1. meetings
2. `1:1` work conversations
3. asking work questions
4. giving status updates
5. confirming deadline / owner / next step
6. reporting issues, blockers, and recommendations

- non-work casual small talk
- long written Slack exchanges

## Strategic filter

Study something only if both are true:

1. A Japanese colleague could notice it in a real work interaction.
2. It is part of the broad common foundation that will still help later when N2 backfill begins.

### Keep

- clarification and confirmation
- deadlines, time, owner, task, next step
- short status updates
- reporting, escalation, and recommendation language
- common spoken grammar for reasons, plans, uncertainty, sequence, completion, and requests
- short spoken summaries of what was understood
- meetings, `1:1`s, and familiar workplace exchanges
- direct technical phrases that avoid unnecessary katakana fallback

### Drop for now

- rare or test-specific N2 grammar distinctions
- long reading passages for their own sake
- heavy kanji study detached from usage
- business email formulas beyond short fixed lines
- company jargon memorization for its own sake
- English-heavy translation catalogs

## Core tutoring rules

### `20-minute` default

Use `20-minute` chunks as the default tutoring unit.

Recommended shape:

- `0:00–3:00` fast recall from memory
- `3:00–10:00` one narrow target only
- `10:00–14:00` contrast / correction loop
- `14:00–18:00` mini role-play, listening recognition, or rapid response
- `18:00–20:00` final output from memory and one improved retry

If a chunk expands because of productive learner questions, that is acceptable, but preserve the core objective.

### Interaction style

- English is for brief explanation and correction only.
- Japanese drives drills, prompts, role-play, shadowing, and final production.
- Give one task at a time.
- Use `3` prompts/questions maximum at once.
- For open prompts, give the intended reply goal in English first, then the Japanese message separately.
- For listening / role-play, give the Japanese message plus a concrete reply goal only.
- If a grammar question appears, answer briefly and return to output fast.
- Keep the production ceiling at direct assertive N3 forms such as `〜します` and `〜して`.
- Do not reintroduce humble corporate loops or retired anchor phrases.

### Japanese-first loop

- Start the session in Japanese.
- Ask for repetition, slower speed, clarification, and confirmation in Japanese when possible.
- Give the closing summary in Japanese first.
- Use Japanese as the default tool for managing Japanese learning whenever it is practical.

### Voice-input and pronunciation policy

- Ignore IME, okurigana, and homophone artifacts when the intended spoken phrase is clear.
- Prioritize spoken grammar, word choice, and workplace naturalness over writing accuracy.
- Do not drill katakana-heavy workplace words when natural Japanese alternatives exist.
- Drill hard technical terms inside full sentences, not as isolated pronunciation exercises.

## Current progress snapshot

Current progress should be understood as active capability, not as chunk count.

### Active capability blocks

- Rescue and clarification language is stable and always available.
- Status updates, ownership confirmation, and next-action questions are deployable from memory.
- Investigation and reporting language is active across unknown cause, likely cause, next steps, follow-up sharing, and root-cause accountability.
- Recommendation language is stable around `この進め方` / `この方針`, `理由は二つあります`, and parallel reason blocks.
- Trade-off and scope-control language is active for phased rollout, provisional scope narrowing, workaround framing, and later permanent-fix routing.
- Cross-functional alignment language is active for schedule synchronization and integration-point gating.
- Risk escalation and containment language is active for obstacle flagging, countermeasures, blast-radius isolation, and impact minimization.
- Operational guardrail language is active for load absorption, quality protection, and stable operations.
- Post-mortem remediation language is active for root-cause accountability, recurrence prevention, and structural cleanup.
- Modernization and deprecation language is active for aging-infrastructure refresh and old-method removal.
- Architectural justification is active for stability-defense phrasing.
- The current next target is strategy-aligned external-product evaluation and cost-effectiveness language built around `技術戦略に則り`、`外部製品の導入を検討して`、and `費用対効果を見極めます`.

### Live difficulty points

- Retrieval speed drops when multiple high-level blocks must be chained under pressure.
- `堅牢性` remains a live pronunciation and pacing pressure point.
- Compact decision-criteria language around `工数`、`影響`、`優先度`、`注意点` on top of the now-stable trade-off framework is still developing.
- Faster unscripted schedule-alignment exchanges are still developing.
- The newer pressure point is chaining operational guardrails, root-cause remediation, and modernization / deprecation at conversational speed without English scaffolding.
- Another live pressure point is avoiding fallback to retired anchors such as `把握しました`、汎用の `進めます`、汎用の `対応`、`〜次第`、and `現状を認識しております`.
