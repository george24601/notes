# Why

## Background

- This is not a tech project. The point is to learn enough Japanese to build trust and relationship with people, especially in workplace interactions.
- The real feedback loop is live communication at work, so the first study block should optimize for speaking and listening performance rather than broad passive knowledge.
- The learner needs Japanese that fits a staff-level architect register: direct, accountable, and usable in architecture reviews, coordination calls, and issue discussions without sounding managerial or over-polite.

## Goals

- Reduce freezing when Japanese is missed or only partially understood.
- Become reliably functional in clarification, status updates, requests, suggestions, blockers, and scheduling.
- Build enough listening ability to follow coherent conversations at nearly natural speed while still staying compatible with JLPT N2 as a secondary benchmark.
- Keep technical workplace phrasing direct and natural without relying on katakana-heavy fallback words.

## Non-Goals

- Long kanji study during this first block.
- Broad reading practice as a primary mode.
- Essay writing, rare grammar coverage, deep keigo theory, or perfect pronunciation.
- Building an English-to-Japanese translation catalog for its own sake.
- Preserving synthetic module or chunk numbering as the main way of tracking progress.

## Tradeoffs

- Favor speaking and listening over exam optimization because the real-world outcome is trust and smoother collaboration, not only a test score.
- Favor fixed chunks and reusable lines over grammar breadth because immediate fluency under pressure matters more than theoretical coverage.
- Favor error logging, recording, and repetition over constant new material because the mistake log is the real curriculum for this phase.
- Use reading mostly to support transcripts, phrase capture, and occasional JLPT check-ins rather than as the center of the study plan.
- Use `20-minute` chunks as the default tutoring unit because live practice produces useful branching clarification questions; those questions should be preserved without letting the calendar model pretend every roadmap item fits cleanly into one hour.
- Keep canonical notes centered on active phrases, repeated mistakes, and next practice pressure; synthetic H/M or M0xx chunk labels are temporary scaffolding, not the memory model.
- Treat short grammar or nuance questions as productive when they stay tied to the target language: answer briefly, then return to speaking practice.
- Do not let review chunks become pure repetition after the target is mostly stable and remaining issues are mostly mistypes; move forward or add exactly one small useful workplace chunk that fits the current theme, then return quickly to production.
- When a narrower pattern is already stable enough, expand to the broader reusable workplace frame instead of over-drilling the narrow contrast; for recommendation language, `この進め方` / `この方針` is higher value than prolonged `第一案` / `第二案` drilling.
- Once `確認`-based language is stable, expand into adjacent workplace action verbs rather than making low-value variants from already-known words; useful next verbs include `見る`, `調べる`, `聞く`, `共有する`, `反映する`, `調整する`, and `行う`.
- During voice-input practice, prioritize spoken grammar, word choice, and workplace naturalness over punctuation, kanji/kana, or Mac IME issues unless writing practice is the explicit goal.
- Do not drill katakana pronunciation or katakana-heavy workplace words and phrases when their meaning is clear or when a natural Japanese alternative is available; the learner lived in North America for `20+` years, so katakana pronunciation drilling has low value and may interfere with English pronunciation.
- For escalation language, prefer workplace Japanese alternatives such as `必要なら上長に相談します` over katakana-heavy phrasing such as `エスカレーションします`.
- For staff+ workplace language, prefer objective framing over personal hesitation. Missing context, decision criteria, concerns, risk, ownership, scope, recommendation framing, and approval-boundary framing are higher-value than phrases like `判断に迷っています`; `判断材料が不足しています` and recommendation-plus-confirmation language are the preferred style.
- For staff+ workplace language, prefer direct declarative ownership over humble corporate loops. The target ceiling is assertive N3 production, not polished executive-keigo performance.
- Do not keep English translations when they do not help planning or correction. The durable memory should preserve Japanese phrases, usage rules, and repeated failure modes.
- Let external planners such as Gemini derive the next session from canonical state instead of storing a fixed next-chunk script in project memory.
- For staff IC coordination language, prefer schedule synchronization and mutual blocking avoidance over manager-style prioritization language aimed at pushing another team.

## Important Historical Context

- Initial phase: the operating plan was a `40-hour` block split into `10 x 4-hour` sessions.
- Foundation phase: the highest-value content areas for that block were clarification language, status update language, request / response language, opinion / suggestion language, scheduling language, polite stock phrases, and listening for function instead of every word.
- Checkpoint phase: JLPT sample questions and the N2 level summary were used only as light checkpoints around the block, not as the main study method.
- Roadmap phase: the plan shifted to a visibility-first `100` hour structure.
- Chunking phase: live tutoring shifted from one-hour sessions to `20-minute` chunks while preserving the same visibility-first roadmap.
- Consolidation phase: the early workplace foundation tightened across clarification, status updates, ownership and next-action confirmation, investigation/reporting, escalation, and recommendation framing.
- Recommendation phase: recommendation language stabilized around `この進め方` / `この方針`, `理由は二つあります`, and parallel `〜ことと、〜ことです` reason blocks; the reduced emphasis on `第一案` / `第二案` should be treated as scope expansion, not regression.
- Trade-off backfill phase: earlier trade-off language was backfilled into canonical state as active capability, including phased rollout, scope narrowing, workaround framing, and later permanent-fix routing.
- Canonical cleanup phase: project-local canonical state was reaffirmed as `current.md`, `why.md`, `open_loops.md`, `phrases.md`, `grammar.md`, and `hard-points.md`; legacy H/M shorthand stayed only in `visibility_first_japanese_100h_master_plan.md`.
- Alignment phase: cross-functional alignment, risk escalation / containment, and first-layer architectural justification were recorded as active workplace capabilities.
- Expansion phase: operational guardrails, post-mortem remediation, and modernization / legacy deprecation were promoted into active capability alongside the earlier staff-level language blocks.
- Latest roadmap phase: modernization / legacy deprecation was closed as active capability, and the next live target moved to strategy-aligned external-product evaluation and cost-effectiveness language.
- Cleanup phase: retired anchors such as `把握しました`、`進めます`、generic `対応`、`〇〇の件`、`第一案`、`〜次第`、and `現状を認識しております` were explicitly removed from active drill surfaces.
- Planning phase: actual planning continues to live in capability, phrase, difficulty, and open-loop surfaces rather than in synthetic lesson IDs.
- Future next-session sequencing should be generated from current capability, open-loop, and difficulty context rather than from synthetic chunk numbering.
