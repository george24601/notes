# Current

## Capability Snapshot

Practical workplace Japanese is active across status sharing, investigation, recommendation framing, trade-off control, cross-functional alignment, risk containment, operational guardrails, post-mortem remediation, modernization, and first-layer architectural defense. The active register is now stricter: keep grammar at an assertive N3 ceiling, avoid humble corporate loops, and prefer non-katakana technical wording when a natural Japanese phrase exists.

Synthetic module and chunk labels are no longer the memory model for this project. Current state is tracked by usable phrases, stable production chains, and repeated mistakes under pressure.

The newest completed area is modernization / deprecation. Workplace-policy grammar is now active: `〜への`、`〜にかかわらず`、`〜なしで`、`〜のうえ`、`速やかに`、`もしくは`、`〜の中でも`、`〜べき`、`〜であったため`、and `〜なので`. The immediate next target is to produce those patterns smoothly in short policy and coordination sentences. Strategy-aligned external-product evaluation remains an active review line:

- `技術戦略に則り、`
- `外部製品の導入を検討して、`
- `費用対効果を見極めます。`

Review pressure remains on `他開発部署と歩調を合わせ、円滑な移行を実現します。` and on keeping long technical chains smooth without falling back to retired anchors such as `把握しました`、`現状を認識しております`、`〜次第`、汎用の `対応`、or 汎用の `進めます`.

## Tutoring Style

- English is for brief explanation and correction only. Japanese should drive all drills, prompts, role-play, and final production.
- Do not dump the whole lesson at once; give one task at a time, 3 prompts max.
- Keep sessions as `20-minute` chunks.
- For listening / role-play: give the Japanese message plus a concrete reply goal only.
- For open prompts: include the intended reply meaning in English first, then the Japanese message separately.
- Ignore voice-input kanji/kana/homophone issues when the intended spoken phrase is clear (see `hard-points.md` for the full list).
- Keep production inside the assertive N3 ceiling: `〜します`、`〜して`、`〜しますので`.
- Avoid humble loops such as `〜したく存じます` and `〜させていただければ幸いです`.
- Avoid katakana-heavy technical defaults when a natural Japanese phrase is available.
- Do not drill retired anchors: `把握しました`、`進めます`、`対応`、`〇〇の件`、`第一案`、`〜次第`、`現状を認識しております`.
- Avoid overusing `確認` as the default action verb; expand into more specific verbs such as `見てみます`、`調べます`、`聞いてみます`、`共有します`、`反映します`、`調整します`.
- Do not drill katakana pronunciation or katakana-heavy words; treat as known when meaning is clear.
- Briefly answer productive grammar questions tied to the target, then return immediately to speaking practice.
- Review chunks should not be pure repetition when the target is stable; either move forward or add exactly one useful adjacent phrase.
- Since practice uses voice input, prioritize spoken grammar, word choice, and workplace naturalness over writing accuracy.
- When closing a session, summarize what became active, what remained hard, and which fallback habits reappeared.
